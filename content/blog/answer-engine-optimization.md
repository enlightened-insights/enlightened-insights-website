<h2 id="what-is-aeo">What is answer engine optimization (AEO)?</h2>
<p>Answer engine optimization (AEO) is the practice of making content eligible, understandable, and easy to cite in direct answers generated by AI systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, and Google AI Overviews. Where SEO aims for a ranked link, AEO aims for inclusion or attribution inside the synthesized answer itself.</p>
<p><a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Search Central</a> (updated July 2026) describes AEO and the overlapping term generative engine optimization (GEO) as work on visibility in AI search experiences, and treats it as part of SEO on Google's own platform. The term GEO comes from <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735" target="_blank" rel="noopener">a paper by Aggarwal, Murahari, et al.</a>, first published in November 2023 and presented at KDD 2024.</p>
<p>Whichever label your audience searches, the work comes down to three things: <strong>extractability</strong> (can an engine lift a passage and have it still make sense?), <strong>sourcing</strong> (does the passage carry named, dated evidence?), and <strong>measurement</strong> (are you tracking whether citations actually happen?).</p>
<h2 id="what-content-gets-cited">What kind of content do answer engines cite?</h2>
<p>Answer engines most consistently cite content that is crawlable, states a direct and complete answer with the subject named, and supports its claims with attributable, dated evidence. Original data, first-hand experience, named statistics, and quotations show the strongest documented associations with citation visibility; generic summaries without sourcing do not.</p>
<h3>Crawlability is the prerequisite, not the differentiator</h3>
<p>Google Search Central requires pages to be indexed and allowed to show a snippet. <a href="https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots" target="_blank" rel="noopener">OpenAI says content must be accessible to <code>OAI-SearchBot</code></a> to appear in ChatGPT search, and <a href="https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Perplexity recommends allowing <code>PerplexityBot</code></a>. Crawlability gets a page considered; whether an engine actually uses it depends on how easy the answer is to find, how clearly the subject is named, and how well the claims are supported.</p>
<h3>What the evidence says about content signals</h3>
<p>In the GEO-bench experiment by Aggarwal et al. (KDD 2024), adding quotations, statistics, or cited sources improved visibility in generative engine responses by up to 40%. The caveat: the experiment gave GPT-3.5 the top five Google results and did not test current ChatGPT or Google AI Overviews.</p>
<p><a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Search Central recommends unique viewpoints, first-hand experience, and original analysis</a>, and says there is no need to split content into tiny "AI-friendly" chunks, no ideal page length, and no special AI schema (updated July 2026). <a href="https://www.conductor.com/academy/how-ai-citations-differ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">A Conductor analysis</a> (updated May 2026) found engines favour different formats: ChatGPT Search leaned toward Wikipedia-style reference content, while Perplexity and Google AI Overviews frequently cited YouTube. Treat that as directional.</p>
<h3>Being cited incorrectly is a real outcome</h3>
<p><a href="https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Tow Center for Digital Journalism</a> tested 1,600 source-identification queries in March 2025 and found incorrect answers in more than 60% of them; ChatGPT Search was wrong on 67% and Perplexity on 37%. It was a narrow news-attribution test, but the lesson carries over: checking whether the AI's statement is actually supported by your page is part of AEO measurement.</p>
<h2 id="more-posts-ai-visibility">Does publishing more blog posts improve AI visibility?</h2>
<p>Publishing more posts does not reliably improve AI visibility on its own. In Ahrefs' study of 75,000 brands (December 2025), the number of pages on a site correlated with AI visibility at only 0.194, while branded web mentions correlated at 0.656 to 0.709 depending on the platform.</p>
<p>Google Search Central says a high quantity of pages does not make a site more relevant, and that creating pages for every fan-out query variation to manipulate results can breach its scaled-content-abuse policy (updated July 2026). Age is not a penalty either: <a href="https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AI-cited pages in Ahrefs' 17-million-citation sample averaged 2.9 years old</a> (Ahrefs, July 2025).</p>
<h3>When to publish and when to update</h3>
<p>Publish when you have no coverage of a question your buyers ask. Update a thin post with original evidence instead of writing a second one. If two of your posts contradict each other, resolve the conflict before either gets cited for the wrong claim.</p>
<p>Our opinion, labelled as such: because brand mentions correlate with AI visibility far more than page count does, a tool that only writes more pages is pointed at a weak lever. Getting original findings in front of journalists, trade publications, and video channels is the stronger one. Google's guidance notes that seeking inauthentic mentions is not as helpful as it might seem, so the mentions have to be earned.</p>
<h2 id="llms-txt">Is llms.txt worth implementing for AEO?</h2>
<p><code>llms.txt</code> is not a meaningful AEO lever in 2026. Google states it does not use the file (Google Search Central, updated July 2026), and no major answer engine has confirmed that it affects retrieval or citation. Fix robots.txt, sitemaps, canonicals, and crawlability first.</p>
<p><a href="https://llmstxt.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><code>llms.txt</code> began as Jeremy Howard's September 2024 proposal</a> for a Markdown site summary; it is not a ratified web standard. <a href="https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ahrefs examined 137,210 domains</a> in May 2026: about 38,000 had a valid <code>llms.txt</code> file, and 97% of those received no bot request at all that month (Ahrefs, June 2026). <a href="https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search.md" target="_blank" rel="noopener">OpenAI's search-crawler documentation</a> points publishers to <code>robots.txt</code> and <code>OAI-SearchBot</code> instead.</p>
<h3>What we fixed on our own site instead</h3>
<p>While preparing this post, we audited enlightenedinsights.org and found no robots.txt, no sitemap, no structured data on blog posts, no canonical tags, and the site answering on both the www and non-www addresses. All of that ships alongside this post. The order we recommend: allow the right crawlers (<code>OAI-SearchBot</code>, <code>PerplexityBot</code>), add sitemaps and canonical tags, and only then consider <code>llms.txt</code>, as a low-cost experiment if your platform generates it for you.</p>
<h2 id="measure-aeo">How do you measure AEO results?</h2>
<p>Measure answer engine optimization (AEO) as a four-layer funnel: technical eligibility (can engines reach the page?), answer visibility (are you cited, on which engines, for which questions?), citation quality (does your page actually support what the AI said?), and business impact (do cited visits convert?). There is no cross-engine equivalent of Search Console yet.</p>
<h3>Platform tools that exist today</h3>
<p><a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google's Search Console generative-AI report</a>, launched in June 2026 and rolled out globally by August 31, 2026, shows impressions by page, date, country, and device for AI Overviews and AI Mode. <a href="https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Microsoft Bing's AI Performance report</a> shows citations, cited pages, and grounding queries, and <a href="https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/6/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Bing added Citation Share in June 2026</a>, which it stresses is not traffic share or a ranking.</p>
<p>For ChatGPT, <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq" target="_blank" rel="noopener">OpenAI's publisher FAQ</a> says referral URLs carry <code>utm_source=chatgpt.com</code>. However, <a href="https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-appears-to-drop-utm-tracking-making-ai-referral-traffic-harder-to-measure-496552" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Search Engine Land reported on October 15, 2026</a> that an Adobe analysis suggests some ChatGPT traffic now arrives without UTM or referrer data. OpenAI had not confirmed the change at the time of writing, so watch direct traffic and landing-page patterns too.</p>
<p>Click data is still forming. <a href="https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Pew Research Center</a> found users clicked a traditional result on 8% of searches with an AI summary versus 15% without one (68,879 searches, July 2025). <a href="https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-search-driving-more-queries-higher-quality-clicks/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google said in August 2025</a> that total organic clicks were relatively stable year over year, but published no figures or methodology.</p>
<h3>Prompt-level monitoring fills the gap</h3>
<p>Run a fixed set of representative prompts across engines and record citation presence, the cited URL, competitors cited, and factual accuracy. Repeat runs, because responses are probabilistic, and log the engine, model, location, and date for each check. We will check this post's target questions in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google at 2, 6, and 13 weeks after publication and update it with what we find.</p>
<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Is generative engine optimization (GEO) the same thing as AEO?</h3>
<p>Largely, yes. Google uses both terms and treats them as extensions of SEO on its own platform. Use whichever term your audience searches; the underlying work (extractability, sourcing, entity clarity, measurement) is the same regardless of label.</p>
<h3>Will adding FAQ schema guarantee AI citations?</h3>
<p>No. Schema helps parsers identify Q&A structure, but citations depend on extractable answers, verifiable evidence, and perceived authority. Build the visible FAQ first and generate the schema from it, not the other way around.</p>
<h3>Do I need to rewrite my whole site for AEO?</h3>
<p>No. Start with the 10 to 20 questions your buyers ask most, build a few pages that answer them with stand-alone, source-backed sections, fix technical crawlability, and confirm the right bots are allowed. Volume is a weak lever; depth on key questions is the stronger one.</p>
<h3>What's the fastest on-page win for AEO?</h3>
<p>Rewrite every section opener to name the subject explicitly (no "it" or "this" to open a section), add a 40 to 60 word direct answer immediately under each question heading, and put the source name and date inside the sentence that makes each statistic.</p>
